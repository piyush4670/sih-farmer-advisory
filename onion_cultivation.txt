TITLE: Onion Cultivation Advisory - Thrips and Purple Blotch Management

LAND PREPARATION
Plough the field 2-3 times to create fine tilth. Incorporate 20-25 tonnes of well-decomposed farmyard manure (FYM) or compost per hectare to boost organic carbon and soil moisture retention.

GROWTH DURATION
Matures in 90-120 days after transplanting, depending on variety and season.

FERTILIZER
Main fertilizers: urea (nitrogen), DAP (phosphorus), MOP (potash). Timing and quantity should follow soil test recommendations - NDVI/remote sensing and local agriculture department advisories can help optimize timing further, but soil testing is the most accessible basis for small farmers.

YIELD EXPECTATION
Typical yield range: 250-300 quintals/hectare (roughly 100-130 quintals/acre), varying by variety, soil, and fertilizer management. Progressive, well-managed fields can reach up to 400 quintals/hectare.

MAJOR PESTS AND TREATMENT

1. Thrips (Thrips tabaci) - the most important onion pest
- Identification: small yellowish insects that suck sap from leaves, causing silver-colored streaks or brown spots on leaves
- More severe in high-humidity conditions and hot, dry weather
- Treatment: spray Azadirachtin 1% at 2 ml/litre (40 days after planting as a preventive), or Dimethoate at 2 ml/litre on a need basis, or neem-based oil formulations
- Note: older recommendations mention Monocrotophos or Methyl Demeton - these are being phased out in favor of safer alternatives; confirm currently approved options with your local KVK

2. Onion Fly (Delia antiqua)
- Identification: small grey fly resembling a house fly; lays eggs in soil around the plant; attack causes lower leaves to yellow
- Treatment: spray with 0.1% Malathion

DISEASES AND TREATMENT

1. Purple Blotch (Alternaria porri) - major onion disease
- Symptoms: irregular purple lesions on leaves, reducing photosynthesis and bulb size; most favorable temperature for disease development is 28-30°C
- Treatment: need-based spray of Tebuconazole at 1.5 ml/litre, or Mancozeb at 2g/litre, or Zineb at 2g/litre
- Organic/bio-control option: foliar spray of Trichoderma harzianum at 1%, or neem-based bio-pesticide (neembicidine) at 0.3% - shown to meaningfully reduce disease severity in field trials
- Prevention: avoid waterlogging and ensure good air circulation in the field

2. Stemphylium Blight
- Often occurs alongside purple blotch, compounding damage
- Similar management approach: fungicide spray plus good field sanitation
- Primary disease spores overwinter in weeds like yellow nutsedge and Datura - keeping field borders weed-free reduces disease carryover between seasons

PRE-HARVEST TIP FOR STORAGE LIFE
Spraying Cycocel at 200 ppm combined with Carbendazim at 1000 ppm, 30 days before harvest, can help extend the shelf life of harvested onion bulbs.

POST-HARVEST HANDLING
Clean and dry harvested bulbs in shade for 4 days before storage. Proper curing and dry storage conditions are essential to prevent storage rot, which is a common cause of post-harvest loss for onion farmers.

COMMON CHALLENGES FOR ONION FARMERS IN INDIA
- Price volatility due to supply-demand imbalances (onion prices are historically very unstable in Indian markets)
- Pest and disease pressure (thrips, purple blotch, storage rot)
- Climate variation - excessive rain or drought can significantly damage the crop
- Lack of proper cold storage leading to post-harvest losses

WHERE TO GET HELP
Contact your local Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK) or ICAR-DOGR (Directorate of Onion and Garlic Research, Rajgurunagar, Pune) for region-specific variety recommendations and current pest/disease treatment guidance.
