TITLE: Wheat Cultivation Advisory - Package of Practices

SOWING TIME (varies by zone)
- North Western Plain Zone (Punjab, Haryana, Delhi, western UP): 1-15 November for timely sowing
- North Eastern Plain Zone: 10-20 November
- Central Zone: 10-20 November
- Peninsular Zone: 1-15 November
- Late sowing: up to third week of December (yields will be lower than timely sown crop)
- Best sowing temperature: around 22°C

SEED RATE AND LAND PREPARATION
- Seed rate: approximately 100 kg/hectare (varies by variety)
- Apply 2-3 tonnes of farmyard manure per hectare, 5-6 weeks before sowing
- Prepare a well-pulverized but compact seed bed with 3-4 ploughings
- For irrigated crop: give a pre-sowing irrigation (palewa), then reduce number of ploughings
- Maintain 20 cm row spacing for timely sowing, 18 cm for late sowing
- Can use Zero Till Drill or Happy Seeder to sow directly into rice stubble (avoids stubble burning)

FERTILIZER DOSE (general recommendation - always prefer soil test-based dosing via Soil Health Card)
For irrigated, timely-sown wheat:
- Nitrogen (N): 120 kg/hectare
- Phosphorus (P2O5): 60 kg/hectare
- Potash (K2O): 40 kg/hectare

For late-sown irrigated wheat (reduced dose):
- Nitrogen: 60-80 kg/hectare
- Phosphorus: 30-40 kg/hectare
- Potash: 20-25 kg/hectare

TIMING OF FERTILIZER APPLICATION
- Apply full dose of phosphorus and potash at the time of sowing (basal application)
- Apply 1/3 of nitrogen at sowing
- Apply remaining nitrogen in split doses: at first irrigation (Crown Root Initiation stage, 20-25 days after sowing) and second irrigation (tillering stage, 40-45 days after sowing)
- Complete all nitrogen application within 40-45 days after sowing
- If crop shows yellowing, do not apply excess nitrogen - get soil tested first

IRRIGATION SCHEDULE (critical growth stages)
1. First irrigation: 20-25 days after sowing (Crown Root Initiation - most critical stage, water stress here severely reduces yield)
2. Second irrigation: 40-45 days after sowing (tillering stage - determines number of grain-bearing spikes)
3. Third irrigation: 60-65 days after sowing (late jointing/stem elongation stage)
4. Fourth irrigation: 85-90 days after sowing (flowering stage - moisture stress here causes poor pollination)
5. Fifth irrigation: 110-115 days after sowing (dough/grain filling stage)
Avoid irrigation if rain is forecast. Furrow-irrigated raised bed method saves 30-40% water compared to flat-bed sowing.

WEED MANAGEMENT
- General weeding: 1.5 to 2 months after sowing
- For broad leaf weeds: spray 2,4-D at 500 ml/acre, or metsulfuron 20 WP at 8 g/acre, or carfentrazone 40 DF at 20 g/acre
- For narrow and broad leaf weeds together: use Sulfosulfuron 75 WG at 13.5 g/acre before first irrigation or 10-15 days after
- For multi-herbicide resistant Phalaris minor (Gulli Danda/Kanaki weed): consult local Krishi Vigyan Kendra for current recommended herbicide, as resistance patterns vary by region

DISEASE WATCH
- Wheat rust: farmers with early-sown crop (October) near poplar trees should watch closely, as rust can start in these conditions first
- Monitor crop regularly, especially in humid/foggy weather

NUTRIENT DEFICIENCY NOTE
Many rice-wheat system soils are deficient in zinc, iron, copper, manganese, molybdenum, boron, and sulphur. If deficiency symptoms appear (yellowing, stunted growth), apply zinc sulphate or gypsum as corrective measure - spray on a clear, bright sunny day for best absorption.

GENERAL ADVICE FOR FARMERS
Always prefer soil-test based fertilizer recommendations over general guidelines. Get your free Soil Health Card done to know your exact soil nutrient status. For pest/disease/herbicide recommendations, always verify current guidance with your local Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK), as recommendations can change based on resistance patterns and new research.

SOURCE: ICAR-Indian Institute of Wheat and Barley Research (Karnal), state agriculture department package of practices.
