TITLE: Tomato Cultivation Advisory - Pest and Disease Management

SOWING/PLANTING TIME
Tomatoes can be grown in both Kharif and Rabi seasons in North India:
- Kharif sowing: July-August
- Rabi sowing: January-February
Crop duration: approximately 90-120 days from transplanting to harvesting.

SOIL AND SPACING
- Best soil: rich, well-drained soil with pH 6-7 (medium-black or sandy loam)
- Plant population: approximately 10,000-12,000 plants per acre
- Maintain 25-50 meter isolation distance between different varieties if saving seed (tomato is self-pollinated)

LAND PREPARATION
Plow the field 2-3 times to make soil fine and loose before transplanting.

FERTILIZER
Use a balanced combination of farmyard manure along with urea (nitrogen), single super phosphate (phosphorus), and muriate of potash (potassium). Exact quantities should be based on soil test results - check with your local Soil Health Card report.

IRRIGATION
- Every 15-20 days in normal season; every 10 days in summer
- Drip irrigation is recommended - saves water and improves fertilizer efficiency
- Avoid overwatering (encourages fungal disease) and avoid water stress (causes flower drop and fruit cracking)

WEED MANAGEMENT
Important in early growth stages. Manual weeding and mulching are effective, since weeds compete with young tomato plants for nutrients, sunlight, and moisture.

MAJOR PESTS AND TREATMENT

1. Fruit Borer (Helicoverpa armigera) - most important pest of tomato
- Damage: larva bores into fruit and feeds on pulp from inside; infested fruit is unfit for consumption
- Treatment: spray Azadirachtin 1.0% EC at 2.0 ml/litre, or Indoxacarb 14.5% SC at 8 ml/10 litre, or Flubendiamide 20 WG at 5g/10 litre, or Novaluron 10% EC at 7.5 ml/10 litre
- Also do mechanical collection and destruction of affected fruits, 3-4 times at periodic intervals
- Use pheromone traps for monitoring

2. Whitefly
- Damage: sucks sap from leaves causing yellowing/curling, transmits leaf curl (mosaic) virus, produces honeydew leading to sooty mold
- Treatment: use yellow sticky traps (12/hectare); if trap activity indicates infestation, spray Dimethoate 30 EC at 2ml/litre; remove severely infected plants; neem-based biopesticides work well for lower infestation levels

3. Aphids and Jassid (Green Leafhopper)
- Damage: suck sap, cause leaf yellowing and curling
- Treatment: remove affected leaves; apply recommended insecticide only if infestation is significant

4. Leaf Miner
- Damage: larva mines between leaf layers making serpentine trails, reduces photosynthesis, causes defoliation
- Monitor closely as extensive damage makes fruits unmarketable

DISEASES AND TREATMENT

1. Early Blight (fungal - Alternaria solani)
- Symptoms: brown circular spots on leaves and fruits, irregular leaf spots starting from leaf margins, appears mainly in vegetative growth phase before flowering
- Treatment: spray Dithane M-45 at 0.2% at 10-15 day intervals, or Chlorothalonil at 0.2% at 8-day intervals
- Prevention: use healthy certified disease-free seed, remove and burn infected crop debris, summer plowing to reduce pathogen survival, minimize humidity in plant canopy

2. Late Blight (fungal - Phytophthora infestans) - most devastating tomato disease
- Symptoms: infects and destroys leaves, stems, and fruits rapidly; spreads fastest in high moisture with moderate temperature (20-25°C)
- Treatment: spray Bordeaux mixture (1:1:100) at fortnightly intervals starting one month after planting; in severe cases use Ridomil/Matko at 2g/litre

3. Powdery Mildew
- Symptoms: white, chalky spots on leaves that spread rapidly, leaves turn yellow and drop
- Treatment: spray 0.25% sulphur solution (25g in 10 litres water), repeat 2-3 times at 10-day intervals

4. Seedling Blight (Damping-off)
- Prevention: treat seeds before sowing with 2g Captan or Carbendazim per kg seed

5. Bacterial Wilt and Viral infections (including leaf curl virus spread by whitefly)
- No direct chemical cure - focus on prevention: use resistant varieties where available, control whitefly vector, remove infected plants immediately to prevent spread

GENERAL DISEASE PREVENTION PRINCIPLE
Crop rotation and choosing resistant varieties are the most cost-effective long-term solutions for disease management, since curative treatment is often difficult once a disease establishes.

IMPORTANT SAFETY NOTE
When using Imidacloprid on tomato, do not repeat spraying after the fruiting stage begins, as this may leave harmful chemical residue in the fruit meant for consumption.

WHERE TO GET HELP
Contact your local Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK) for current pest/disease pressure in your area and updated treatment recommendations, since chemical availability and resistance patterns change over time.

SOURCE: ICAR-National Research Centre for Integrated Pest Management (NCIPM), state horticulture department guidelines.
