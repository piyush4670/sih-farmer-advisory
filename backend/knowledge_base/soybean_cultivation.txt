TITLE: Soybean Cultivation Advisory

SEASON AND SOWING TIME
Soybean is primarily a Kharif (rainy season) crop in India:
- Sowing: onset of monsoon, last week of June to first week of July
- Harvesting: September-October
- Crop duration: approximately 90-110 days depending on variety and climate
- A smaller spring-sown crop is also grown in some regions (15th February to 15th March)

SOIL SELECTION
Sandy loam and loamy soils with high organic carbon content are best suited for soybean.

LAND PREPARATION
- Deep ploughing once every 2-3 years to maintain soil moisture and nutrient balance
- After monsoon onset, level field with cultivator 2-3 times followed by planking
- During final ploughing, incorporate 5-10 tonnes of well-decomposed farmyard manure or 2.5 tonnes poultry manure per hectare

SOWING METHOD
- Line sowing using seed drill is preferred (uses fewer seeds, easier weeding/hoeing)
- Spacing: 45-60 cm x 2.5 cm for Kharif season
- Seedling depth: 2-3 cm in heavy soils, 3-4 cm in light soils
- Seed rate: 20-30 kg/hectare for grain purpose (70-75 kg/hectare if grown for fodder)
- Seeds should have minimum 70% germination rate - if lower, increase seed rate accordingly
- Seed treatment: treat with Rhizobium culture before sowing (helps nitrogen fixation), and with fungicides like Thiram or Carbendazim to prevent seed-borne diseases

FERTILIZER DOSE
Since soybean is a legume, symbiotic nitrogen fixation by root nodules supplies part of its nitrogen need, but studies show soybean still responds positively to applied nitrogen - the crop is supplied only 10-15% of its total nitrogen requirement from its own root fixation.
- Nitrogen: 8-50 kg/hectare (lower end if good nodulation, higher if needed)
- Phosphorus (P2O5): 32-100 kg/hectare
- Potash (K2O): 8 kg/hectare
- Sulphur: 8-20 kg/hectare
- Zinc Sulphate: 25 kg/acre for deep black soil, 10 kg/acre for shallow soil (apply after every 5-6 crop cycles, not every season)
- Borax: 10 kg/hectare where deficiency noted

IRRIGATION
Kharif soybean is generally rainfed and does not need irrigation unless rainfall is insufficient. If irrigation becomes necessary (or for spring-sown crop), critical growth stages needing water are:
1. Sprouting stage
2. Flowering stage
3. Pod initiation stage
4. Bean/seed filling stage
These stages are most critical for final yield - prioritize irrigation here if water is limited.

WEED MANAGEMENT
Critical during first 30-40 days after sowing. Options:
- Mechanical: harrow or rotavator after germination
- Pre-sowing herbicide: Fluchloralin 800 ml/acre
- Pre-emergence herbicides: Alachlor 1.6 L/acre, Pendimethalin 1.2 L/acre, or Metolachlor 800 ml/acre
- Post-emergence (for grass and broadleaf weeds): Quizalofop-ethyl 400 ml/acre, or Imazethapyr 300 ml/acre

MAJOR PESTS
- Blue beetle
- Leaf-eating caterpillars
- Stem fly
- Girdle beetle
- Aphids

PEST MANAGEMENT APPROACH
- Do soil and seed treatment before and at planting as a preventive step
- Biological control options: Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt), Beauveria bassiana, or NPV (Nuclear Polyhedrosis Virus) sprays - most effective when applied during early pest stages
- Monitor regularly and use recommended chemical control only when pest levels are significant

MAJOR DISEASE
Rust - a fungal disease that can significantly affect soybean; use recommended fungicide and resistant varieties where available; consult local KVK for current recommendation.

RECOMMENDED VARIETIES (region-specific - ICAR/IARI identified)
- Northern Plains (e.g., Delhi region): Pusa 12, SL 952, NRC 130
- Central Region (e.g., Madhya Pradesh): JS 20-34, JS 116, JS 335, NRC 128
Farmers should confirm the best variety for their specific agro-climatic zone with their local KVK or ICAR center, as ICAR has released 25 improved climate-smart soybean varieties overall.

BENEFITS OF SOYBEAN IN CROP ROTATION
As a legume, soybean fixes atmospheric nitrogen and improves soil fertility for the next crop, making it valuable in sustainable crop rotation systems, not just for its own yield.

GENERAL ADVICE
Test soil before sowing to understand nutrient deficiencies and apply fertilizer precisely rather than using blanket recommendations. Use certified, pest-resistant seed suited to your agro-climatic zone.

WHERE TO GET HELP
Visit your nearest ICAR center or Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK) for information on latest improved soybean varieties and current regional pest/disease guidance.

SOURCE: ICAR/IARI soybean advisories, state agriculture department Kharif guidelines.
