TITLE: Rice (Paddy) Cultivation Advisory - Package of Practices

SOWING/TRANSPLANTING TIME
- Best sowing window in India: late May to early June, depending on region and monsoon arrival
- Nursery seedlings are ready for transplanting 25-30 days after sowing (or when seedlings are 15-21 days old / 25-30 cm long, depending on method)
- In high altitude areas (above 1300m), transplanting should be completed by 15th June, preferably first week of June, to avoid low temperature during flowering

SOIL AND WATER REQUIREMENTS
- Best soil: clay loam with good water-holding capacity and proper drainage
- Ideal soil pH: 5.5 to 7.0
- Total water requirement: 1,200-1,500 mm during the entire growing season

NURSERY PREPARATION
- Nursery area needed: about 1/10 of the area to be transplanted (for conventional method)
- Treat seeds before sowing: soak in water for 8 hours, then treat with Trichoderma dust formulation at 10g/kg of seed (helps prevent seed-borne diseases)
- In zinc-deficient soil, apply zinc sulphate to the nursery as a basal dose
- Irrigate nursery regularly - insufficient moisture damages seedlings

FERTILIZER DOSE (general guideline - always prefer soil test-based recommendation)
Common recommended dose: approximately 130 kg/hectare Nitrogen, 50 kg/hectare Phosphorus, 40 kg/hectare Potassium (varies by region and soil type)

Main fertilizers used: Urea (Nitrogen), DAP or SSP (Phosphorus), MOP - Muriate of Potash (Potassium), and Zinc Sulphate as needed

TIMING OF FERTILIZER APPLICATION (split nitrogen application is important to reduce losses)
- Apply full Phosphorus and Potash, plus 50% of Nitrogen, as basal dose at transplanting
- Apply 25% Nitrogen at maximum tillering stage
- Apply remaining 25% Nitrogen at panicle initiation stage
Splitting nitrogen this way improves nitrogen use efficiency and crop yield compared to applying it all at once.

WEED MANAGEMENT
- Remove weeds during the first 30-45 days after planting - this is the critical window
- Maintain proper standing water levels to naturally suppress weed growth
- Use recommended herbicides only when necessary, following label instructions
- Practice crop rotation to reduce recurring weed problems
- Combine cultural, mechanical, and chemical methods for best long-term weed control (Integrated Weed Management)

PEST MANAGEMENT - STEM BORER AND LEAF FOLDER
- Use pheromone traps for monitoring: 3 traps per acre in nursery
- Take action when trap catch reaches 4-5 male moths per trap
- Treatment options: Azadirachtin 0.15% EC at 800 ml/acre, or Chlorantraniliprole 0.4% GR at 4kg/acre (mixed with sand 1:1), or Chlorantraniliprole 18.5% SC at 60 ml/acre in 200 litres water, or Cartap hydrochloride 4G at 10kg/acre

PEST MANAGEMENT - CASE WORM
- Spray Indoxacarb 15.8% EC at 80 ml/acre, or Flubendiamide-based products (check current local KVK recommendation for exact product/dose)

GENERAL PEST/DISEASE APPROACH
- Monitor fields regularly for early identification
- Remove affected plants where possible
- Use crop protection products only when pest levels cross economic threshold (not preventively) - this reduces cost and chemical overuse
- Weakened plants from poor nutrition are more prone to pest and disease infestation - balanced fertilization also acts as prevention

MODERN ALTERNATIVE METHODS
- Direct Seeded Rice (DSR): saves labor and water compared to transplanting, suited for water-scarce areas
- System of Rice Intensification (SRI): uses only 5-8 kg seed/hectare (vs 40-50 kg normally), single young seedlings planted with wide spacing, intermittent watering; can show 20-50% yield increase in trials but is labor-intensive for weeding

HARVESTING
Harvest when 80-85% of grains have turned golden yellow, grains are hard, and moisture content is around 20-25%. Timely harvesting reduces grain losses and maintains quality.

GENERAL ADVICE FOR FARMERS
Always base fertilizer application on soil test results (via free Soil Health Card scheme) rather than general guidelines alone. For current pest/disease treatment recommendations, verify with your local Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK), as products and doses may be updated.

SOURCE: ICAR-National Rice Research Institute, state agriculture department package of practices.
