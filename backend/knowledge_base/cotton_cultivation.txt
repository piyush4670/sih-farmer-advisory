TITLE: Cotton Cultivation Advisory - Focus on Pink Bollworm Management

GENERAL CULTIVATION PRACTICES
- Timely sowing of short to medium duration Bt-cotton hybrids/varieties is a key defense against pink bollworm - delayed sowing increases infestation risk
- Proper plant spacing and balanced nutrient management improve crop resilience against pests and diseases
- Foliar fertilization (e.g., potassium nitrate spray) can help support the crop during bloom stage

PINK BOLLWORM (PBW) - THE MAJOR COTTON PEST IN INDIA
- Scientific name: Pectinophora gossypiella
- This pest bores into the cotton boll and feeds on blossoms and seeds from inside - it CANNOT be seen from outside, making it hard to detect
- Infestation typically observed 45-60 days after sowing in Bt cotton
- Has become a major threat across cotton growing states, especially in central and southern India, in recent years
- Severity varies state to state

MONITORING (essential for timely action)
- Install pheromone traps at 5 traps per hectare, starting 45 days after sowing
- Continue monitoring moth activity throughout the season
- Take action based on Economic Threshold Level (ETL): 8 male moths per trap per night, OR 10% infested green bolls
- Do not spray on a fixed schedule - only when ETL is crossed, this avoids unnecessary chemical use and cost

INTEGRATED MANAGEMENT STRATEGY (ICAR-CICR recommendations)
1. Timely sowing of recommended Bt-cotton varieties
2. Use of refuge crop (non-Bt cotton planted alongside, as per seed packet instructions) - delays resistance development
3. Regular pheromone trap monitoring
4. After 60 days of sowing: spray neem seed extract 5% + neem oil mixture as an early, less harmful intervention
5. Biological control: Trichogramma (a beneficial parasitic wasp) can be released at 60,000/acre, at 3-week intervals, between October and December, based on availability
6. Use only recommended and approved insecticides - do not mix insecticides
7. Avoid these insecticides in the first 3 months of the crop: Monocrotophos, Acephate, Imidacloprid, Thiamethoxam (these harm natural predators and can worsen pest resistance)
8. Field sanitation - remove and destroy crop residue after harvest to reduce pest carryover to next season
9. Timely termination of the crop (don't let it stand too long after harvest) - this breaks the pest's life cycle

OTHER SUCKING PESTS TO MONITOR ALONGSIDE PBW
- Thrips, whitefly, and leafhopper (jassid) also affect cotton and should be monitored using ETL guidelines, since focusing only on bollworm can let these pests build up
- Whitefly ETL: approximately 8-10 per 3 leaves (verify current threshold with local KVK)

COTTON DISEASES TO WATCH
- Cotton Leaf Curl Disease (viral, spread by whitefly) - graded I to IV in severity
- Fungal foliar leaf spots - can appear alongside pest damage, consult KVK for fungicide recommendation if observed

WHY IPM WORKS BETTER THAN CHEMICAL-ONLY APPROACH
Field studies show IPM-based cotton management reduces the number of insecticide sprays needed (by around 50%) while increasing yield (by around 28%) compared to farmers' conventional practice, along with better economic returns. This is because IPM conserves natural predators and parasitoids in the field, which help control pests naturally.

IMPORTANT WARNING
Farmers should only purchase authenticated/genuine agri-inputs (seeds, pesticides) from verified sources - counterfeit products are a real risk and can fail to protect the crop while wasting money.

WHERE TO GET HELP
Contact your local Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK) or ICAR-CICR (Central Institute for Cotton Research, Nagpur) for current, region-specific recommendations, as pest pressure and resistance patterns change season to season.

SOURCE: ICAR-CICR (Central Institute for Cotton Research), ICAR-NCIPM field validation studies.
