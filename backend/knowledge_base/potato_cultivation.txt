TITLE: Potato Cultivation Advisory - Late Blight Management

IMPORTANCE OF POTATO IN INDIAN AGRICULTURE
Potato contributes about 2.48% of total agricultural output from just 1.07% of total cropped area - roughly 4 times more economic contribution per unit area than wheat or rice. Major growing states: West Bengal, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Gujarat.

PLANTING TIME AND METHOD
Tubers are typically planted on top of ridges in the first week of October (varies by region - hill areas and plains have different windows). Maintain spacing of approximately 50 cm between rows and 15 cm between plants.

FERTILIZER DOSE (general guideline - confirm with local soil test)
- Nitrogen (N): 150 kg/hectare
- Phosphorus (P): 100 kg/hectare
- Potassium (K): 100 kg/hectare
- Lime: 500 kg/hectare (especially useful in acidic hill soils)

TIMING OF FERTILIZER APPLICATION
- Apply half the Nitrogen dose along with full Phosphorus and Potassium as basal dose at time of sowing
- Apply remaining half of Nitrogen as top dressing at 30 days after sowing

EARTHING UP
Perform earthing up (mounding soil around the base of plants) twice during the season - typically at 25 and 40 days after sowing. This supports tuber development and protects developing tubers from sunlight (which causes greening).

IRRIGATION
Give light irrigation shortly after planting (around 6 days after sowing) to support early growth, then continue as per soil moisture needs.

LATE BLIGHT (Phytophthora infestans) - MOST DESTRUCTIVE POTATO DISEASE
- This is a fungus-like pathogen that spreads very rapidly and can infect the crop at any growth stage
- Symptoms appear on leaves, petiole, stem, and tubers - can cause complete defoliation and severe yield loss if not managed in time
- Spreads fastest in cool, humid weather conditions

FUNGICIDE SPRAY SCHEDULE (integrated approach shown effective in field trials)
- First spray: Mancozeb 75% at 0.25%, around 35 days after sowing
- Second spray: Cymoxanil 8% + Mancozeb 64% combination at 0.25%, around 42 days after sowing
- Additional sprays with Mandipropamid 23.4% SC at 0.1% (35 & 55 days after sowing) or Ametoctradin + Dimethomorph combination at 0.1% (45 & 65 days after sowing) have shown strong results in reducing disease incidence
- Always follow current CIBRC-approved fungicide list and local KVK spray schedule, as recommendations are refined based on regional weather patterns each season

INTEGRATED LATE BLIGHT MANAGEMENT (combine all of these for best results)
1. Remove sources of infection - eliminate cull potatoes (leftover tubers from previous season) and volunteer plants before new planting
2. Plant only healthy, disease-free seed tubers from a reliable source
3. Use resistant potato varieties where available (e.g., Kufri Himalini is a late-blight resistant variety suited for hill cultivation)
4. Scout the field regularly, especially in locations where the disease typically appears first (low-lying, shaded, or high-humidity spots)
5. Combine fungicide spraying with these cultural practices - relying on chemical spray alone is less effective and increases cost and environmental impact
6. Weather-based forecasting (tracking humidity and temperature) can help time sprays more precisely - ask your local KVK if this advisory service is available in your area

GENERAL ADVICE FOR FARMERS
Potato late blight can spread extremely fast once conditions favor it (cool, humid weather) - early detection through regular field scouting is more valuable than waiting for visible large-scale damage before acting.

WHERE TO GET HELP
Contact ICAR-Central Potato Research Institute (CPRI), Shimla, or your local Krishi Vigyan Kendra for region-specific late blight forecasting and current fungicide recommendations.

SOURCE: ICAR-Central Potato Research Institute (Shimla), ICAR-National Research Centre for Integrated Pest Management field studies.
